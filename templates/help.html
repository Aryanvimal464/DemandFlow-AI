1. What is SupplyMind AI?

SupplyMind AI is a decision-support dashboard for a company that buys multigrain and spices from suppliers and sells them to dealers. Its main job is to answer three questions for every product:

How much to order?

Reorder quantity from the supplier so stock does not run out or pile up.

When to order?

Based on demand, current stock, incoming stock and supplier lead time.

How much to supply now?

How much stock to ship to dealers against their pending orders.

All data (products, dealers, orders, costs) is synthetic. Savings, service level and accuracy are simulation outputs, not real business results.

6. How the AI learns (Q-learning)

The agent keeps a table called the Q-table. It has 27 situations (states) and 5 possible actions. Each cell is a score: "how good is this action in this situation in the long run?"

Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α · [ r + γ · max Q(s',a') − Q(s,a) ]

State (27)

Stock cover (LOW / NORMAL / HIGH) × demand level (LOW / NORMAL / HIGH) × pending orders (NONE / SOME / HIGH).

Actions (5)

ORDER_MORE (2× weekly demand), SUPPLY_HIGH (1.5×), SUPPLY_NORMAL (1×), WAIT, REDUCE_STOCK.

Rewards

+1 correct decision, avoided stockout, demand satisfied. −0.5 excess stock, unnecessary reorder. −1 stockout, late supply. −0.3 when you reject a recommendation.

Explore vs exploit

While training, the agent sometimes tries random actions (epsilon) to discover better ones. Epsilon shrinks to 0.05, so later it mostly uses what it learned.

Before vs after

Before = random decisions. After = the learned policy. Both are tested on the same 300 seeded episodes, so the comparison is fair.

The final quantity is not only the raw Q-table action: it is also adjusted to reach safety stock plus lead-time demand, and capped at the maximum stock level.
